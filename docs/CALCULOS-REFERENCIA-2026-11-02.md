# Calibrate your WHOOP clone to WHOOP's numbers

WHOOP's exact algorithms are proprietary, and for most metrics the decisive constants (Recovery weights and baseline kernel, Strain's intensity thresholds and squash parameters, sleep-debt carryover, the staging model) have never been published, so no amount of reading will give you a byte-exact reimplementation. What *is* public is more than most clone builders use: WHOOP's patents contain real equations (a 0/1/18/42-weighted heart-rate-reserve integral for Strain, normalized by 24 hours and squashed with an arctangent; `SleepNeed = Baseline + f1(strain) + f2(debt) − Naps` with `f1(i) = 1.7/(1+e^((17−i)/3.5))`), WHOOP's Data Science lead described the current HRV window in 2025 (overlapping 5-minute RMSSD windows stepped every 30 s, wake removed, weighted toward slow-wave and late-night sleep), the API exposes intermediates such as the four Sleep Need components and per-zone durations, and the Healthspan white paper publishes WHOOP Age almost completely (9 contributors, hazard ratio to years via `10·ln(HR)`, explicit thresholds and a VO2max target table). One new check strengthens the patent evidence considerably: plugging the API documentation's example Day Strain (5.2951527) into the patented sleep-need strain term, read in **hours**, returns 208,595 ms, exactly the documentation's example `need_from_recent_strain_milli`. Your clone almost certainly diverges for a handful of structural reasons (calendar days instead of sleep-to-sleep cycles, plain full-night RMSSD, raw instead of baseline-normalized inputs, 220−age max HR, Edwards zones with an arbitrary `ln(7201)` scale, ignoring muscular load, and computing Sleep Performance as pure sufficiency after WHOOP blended it), and every open-source WHOOP clone shares these flaws and admits it is uncalibrated. The way to close the remaining gap is to fix the structural issues first, then fit the unknown constants per user against WHOOP's own API and CSV export, metric by metric, using the calibration plan at the end of this report.

## What WHOOP has actually disclosed, and what it computes from

Every score is computed in WHOOP's cloud, not on the strap. US11574722B2 claims a remote server that calculates continuous HR, determines resting heart rate at least daily, calculates HRV, converts HR into a heart-rate-reserve time series, detects exercise, and generates an intensity score "using a weighted sum of heart rate reserve data" ([US11574722B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11574722B2/en)). The strap uploads roughly 1 Hz history records containing HR, a variable count of RR intervals, raw green and red/IR PPG ADC values, accelerometer triplets, a skin-contact flag, signal quality, and raw SpO2, skin-temperature and respiration ADCs, which WHOOP converts server-side ([NOOP protocol map](https://github.com/muftiarfan/noop/blob/main/Packages/WhoopProtocol/Sources/WhoopProtocol/Resources/whoop_protocol.json)). The openwhoop code notes that "whoop doesn't always measure rr" in history records ([openwhoop stress.rs](https://github.com/ThomasBoutelier/openwhoop/blob/master/src/openwhoop/src/algo/stress.rs)). If your clone works from strap-reported RR intervals or from a third-party HR stream, **you are starting with less data than WHOOP**, which re-derives beats from raw PPG and gates each window with a learned quality probability trained against chest straps ([US11185292B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11185292B2/en)). This alone guarantees some residual HRV mismatch.

Confidence labels in this report follow one scheme. **Official** means WHOOP's API spec, white paper, support/Locker text or a WHOOP staff statement. **Patent** means WHOOP patent text, which describes embodiments and does not prove production use (claims carry more weight than "e.g." passages). **Clone** means a third-party reimplementation's choice, not WHOOP's. **Inferred** means reasoning from the above. **Unknown** means nothing reliable exists. Many official claims come from search-engine extracts of whoop.com and support.whoop.com pages, which block automated fetching; treat those as slightly weaker. The API is now v2 (`v1_id` "Will not exist past 09/01/2025") ([WHOOP OpenAPI](https://api.prod.whoop.com/developer/doc/openapi.json)).

The day unit is the foundation for everything else. WHOOP's "physiological cycle" **begins when you fall asleep and resets the next time you fall asleep**, not at midnight, and past cycle boundaries "may change for a few days" as WHOOP reprocesses ([WHOOP Locker](https://www.whoop.com/ie/en/thelocker/your-top-questions-answered); [WHOOP engineering](https://engineering.prod.whoop.com/dev-platform-2)). Cycle `kilojoule` and `strain` are totals over that variable-length window, and records carry `updated_at`, so an export pulled weeks later can differ from what the app showed that morning.

## Recovery is a baseline-relative composite dominated by a weighted HRV window

### HRV: replace full-night RMSSD with WHOOP's weighted window scheme

The current method comes from Torey, WHOOP's Data Science lead, writing in August 2025: "we calculate HRV in overlapping 5-minute windows (stepping forward every 30 seconds) throughout your entire sleep. We remove periods of wake, then take a weighted average of the remaining windows," with weights "higher for intervals that occur during likely slow-wave sleep" and for intervals later in the night. He called the older "last slow-wave sleep" description "a bit outdated" and admitted unusually high variability "can still impact the score more than we'd like" ([WHOOP Community](https://www.community.whoop.com/t/hrv-extreme-spikes/6472?page=2)). The patented pre-2025 method took HRV at the end of the most recent SWS period before waking, in "a window of predetermined duration … having a highest quality of heart rate data" ([US9743848B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US9743848B2/en); [US9750415B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US9750415B2/en)), and the Bellenger et al. validation compared the final 5 minutes of the final SWS episode, finding that WHOOP's own interval filter and a Kubios "Strong" (200 ms) artifact filter gave the best agreement with ECG ([PMC8160717](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8160717/)). The API field `hrv_rmssd_milli` is **linear RMSSD in milliseconds**, not ln(RMSSD) ([WHOOP OpenAPI](https://api.prod.whoop.com/developer/doc/openapi.json)). Confidence: **official structure, unknown weights**.

```
ibi = clean(ppg_beats)                 # drop RR outside 300–2000 ms, ectopics >20% from local median,
                                       # and successive diffs >200 ms (Kubios "Strong" proxy)
windows = sliding(ibi, len=300s, step=30s, span=main_sleep)
keep    = [w for w in windows if stage(w) != WAKE and quality(w) >= q_min]
weight(w) = (1 + a * P_sws(w)) * g(t_rel(w))     # a > 0, g increasing over the night; a, g UNKNOWN -> fit
HRV = sum(weight*rmssd) / sum(weight)            # linear vs log averaging UNKNOWN -> test both
```

A plain full-night RMSSD typically comes out **higher and noisier** than WHOOP's value because REM and early-night windows are more variable. Open-source clones do exactly that: openwhoop averages each second's RR to one value and takes the plain mean of 300-sample rolling RMSSDs, with no artifact filter, wake removal or SWS weighting ([openwhoop sleep.rs](https://github.com/ThomasBoutelier/openwhoop/blob/master/src/openwhoop/src/algo/sleep.rs)). Because SWS weighting depends on staging, WHOOP's February 2025 staging update could have nudged HRV despite WHOOP saying it would not ([Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/New-Whoop-update-makes-sleep-staging-7-more-accurate.969194.0.html)).

### RHR, respiratory rate, SpO2 and skin temperature

No source gives the current RHR method. Older support text placed RHR in the same last-SWS period as HRV ([WHOOP Support](https://support.whoop.com/hc/en-us/articles/360019453654-How-is-Recovery-Calculated-)); the best inference is the same weighted-window logic applied to mean HR, though NOOP's alternative (the minimum of non-overlapping 5-minute HR means inside sleep) is a reasonable candidate to test ([NOOP RecoveryScorer](https://github.com/muftiarfan/noop/blob/main/Packages/StrandAnalytics/Sources/StrandAnalytics/RecoveryScorer.swift)). Confidence: **inferred**. Respiratory rate is derived from **respiratory sinus arrhythmia** in the heart-rate signal and reported as "the median respiratory rate during sleep," within one breath per minute of gold standard per WHOOP's cited study ([WHOOP Locker](https://www.whoop.com/thelocker/respiratory-rate-tracking-coronavirus/)); extract the dominant IBI frequency in 0.15–0.4 Hz over sleep epochs and take the median. API values are multiples of 1/256 (the documented 16.11328125 equals 4125/256), suggesting fixed-point output, so round your comparison accordingly. SpO2 and skin temperature exist only on 4.0 and later; the developer docs say they are "factored into" Recovery ([WHOOP developer docs](https://developer.whoop.com/docs/developing/user-data/recovery)), while the Health Monitor page treats them as separate deviation alerts that can be green while Recovery is not ([WHOOP Locker](https://www.whoop.com/thelocker/health-monitor-feature/)). Treat them as low-weight or penalty terms. The clone formulas `SpO2 = 110 − 25·R` and `temp = raw × 0.04` are textbook placeholders, not WHOOP calibrations ([scandolo/openwhoop](https://github.com/scandolo/openwhoop)).

### Recovery: the baseline and the mapping are the unknowns that matter

WHOOP's patents call Recovery "a weighted combination of the heart rate variability, the resting heart rate, the intensity score, and the sleep quality indicator" ([US11574722B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11574722B2/en)) and describe comparing 7-day and 3-day moving averages with smoothing to separate real change from noise ([US9743848B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US9743848B2/en)). Respiratory rate became the "fourth pillar" in 2020 but "often doesn't impact your recovery" ([WHOOP Podcast 84](https://whoop.com/thelocker/podcast-84-recovery-update); [WHOOP Recovery 101](https://whoop.com/thelocker/how-does-whoop-recovery-work-101)). The only confirmed baseline fact is a **4-day calibration** during which Recovery is grayed out and `user_calibrating=true` ([WHOOP Podcast 40](https://www.whoop.com/thelocker/podcast-40-whoop-recovery-maximize-readiness/)). An unverified support extract says Recovery uses "a weighted 28-day average, with greater emphasis on recent trends" ([WHOOP Support](https://support.whoop.com/hc/en-us/articles/360019453454-WHOOP-Recovery)); community sources cite 14 or about 30 days ([WHOOP Community](https://www.community.whoop.com/t/feedback-on-recovery-scoring-and-cycle-related-physiological-changes/10322)). Zones are **green 67–100, yellow 34–66, red 0–33**, matching the patent's 66/33 cut points ([US9743848B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US9743848B2/en)). WHOOP stresses that two people with identical HRV, RHR, respiratory rate and sleep performance "could get very different Recoveries" ([WHOOP Support](https://support.whoop.com/hc/en-us/articles/360019453654-How-is-Recovery-Calculated-)), which rules out any fixed absolute-threshold model.

The best public evidence on weights is one user's 475-day regression, where `Recovery ~ HRV + RHR + sleep` gave **+0.98 points per ms of HRV, −0.49 per bpm of RHR, +2.27 per hour of sleep**, R² ≈ 0.76 with a residual SE near 10 points ([RPubs](https://rstudio-pubs-static.s3.amazonaws.com/896715_0cb97b951bcf40e0ac3b7bf5ffcd01d9.html)). Raw-unit coefficients are person-specific, and the unexplained 20–25% points to baseline normalization and nonlinearity. A defensible template, which is a design inference with every constant to be fitted, follows the clones' structure but leaves the weights free:

```
base_x   = EWMA over prior nights only (exclude tonight), half-life h ≈ 7–14 d, window ≤ 28 d
spread_x = robust EWMA of |x − base_x| × 1.253, winsorized at 3σ
z_hrv  = (ln HRV − base_lnHRV)/spread ;  z_rhr = −(RHR − base)/spread ;  z_rr = −|RR − base|/spread
z_sleep = (sleep_perf − 0.85)/0.12  (or hours asleep vs need)
Z = w1 z_hrv + w2 z_rhr + w3 z_sleep + w4 z_rr [+ w5 z_temp + w6 z_spo2]
Recovery = 100 · σ(k·(Z − z0))   # or 100·Φ(Z); clip 0–100; grey out for first 4 nights
```

For orientation only, NOOP uses `wHRV 0.60, wRHR 0.20, wResp 0.05, wSleep 0.15, k 1.6, z0 −0.20` ([NOOP RecoveryScorer](https://github.com/muftiarfan/noop/blob/main/Packages/StrandAnalytics/Sources/StrandAnalytics/RecoveryScorer.swift)) and GenieMax uses `100·Φ(0.55 zHRV − 0.20 zRHR − 0.10 zRR + 0.15 zSleep)` on ln(HRV) against a baseline computed before tonight ([geniemax-core](https://github.com/satayutata/geniemax-core)); both say they are not WHOOP-identical. Confidence: **structure official/patent; weights, kernel and mapping unknown**. In a clone, the HRV window and the baseline kernel cause far more mismatch than SpO2 or skin temperature.

## Strain is a weighted heart-rate-reserve integral, not Edwards zones on a log

### The patent equation clones ignore

WHOOP officially describes Strain as a personalized, non-linear 0–21 score from time in max-HR-based zones, with higher zones weighted more, inspired by the Borg scale (light 0–9, moderate 10–13, high 14–17, all out 18–21), and harder to build near the top ([WHOOP Locker](https://www.whoop.com/thelocker/how-does-whoop-strain-work-101/); [WHOOP Support](https://support.whoop.com/s/article/WHOOP-Strain?language=en_US)). Day Strain is not the sum of activity strains ([WHOOP Locker](https://www.whoop.com/au/en/thelocker/how-does-whoop-strain-work-101)), and the API reports it as an unrounded float ([WHOOP OpenAPI](https://api.prod.whoop.com/developer/doc/openapi.json)). Two official details narrow the model: **changing max HR changes Strain, but changing displayed zone boundaries does not** ([WHOOP Locker](https://www.whoop.com/thelocker/calculating-max-heart-rate); [WHOOP Locker](https://www.whoop.com/thelocker/max-heart-rate-training-zones)). The displayed zones are therefore not the internal weighting.

The internal weighting appears in WHOOP's patent family as an embodiment ([US12318226B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12318226B2/en)): `v(t) = (H(t) − RHR)/(MHR − RHR)`; `w(v) = 0` at v = 0, **1 for v in (0, AT], 18 for (AT, CPT], 42 for (CPT, 1]**, where AT is the anaerobic threshold and CPT the creatine-phosphate threshold; `I_T = ∫ w(v(t)) dt`; `N_T = I_T / (w(1)·24 h)`; and an "exemplary scaling scheme" `f(x, N, p) = 0.5·(arctan(N(x − p))/(π/2) + 1)`, multiplied by 21. The patent also allows fitting the curve to "canonical" exercise routines, sigmoid or other squashes, and logistic-regression weights. N, p, AT and CPT are **unknown**. Confidence: **patent embodiment**.

```
v   = clip((HR − RHR_14d)/(HRmax_whoop − RHR_14d), 0, 1)     # per second
w   = 0 if v<=v0 else 1 if v<=AT else 18 if v<=CPT else 42    # AT, CPT, v0 UNKNOWN -> fit (try AT≈0.6–0.75, CPT≈0.85–0.9)
x   = sum(w * dt) / (1 * 86400 s)                              # integrate over the CYCLE (sleep onset to sleep onset)
cv  = 21 * [f(x) − f(0)]/[f(1) − f(0)],  f(x)=0.5*(atan(N*(x−p))/(pi/2)+1)    # N, p UNKNOWN -> fit
Day Strain = combine(cv, msk_load)                             # combination rule UNKNOWN (not additive)
Activity Strain = same pipeline over the workout window
```

Whether the floor `f(0)` is subtracted is not stated; test it, since resting time must map near zero. Every open-source clone instead uses Edwards zone weights 1–5 at 50/60/70/80/90 %HRR and `Strain = 21·ln(TRIMP+1)/ln(7201)`, where 7201 is a theoretical ceiling (24 h at max HR) "not" fitted to WHOOP ([scandolo strain.py](https://github.com/scandolo/openwhoop/blob/main/openwhoop/algos/strain.py); [NOOP StrainScorer](https://github.com/muftiarfan/noop/blob/main/Packages/StrandAnalytics/Sources/StrandAnalytics/StrainScorer.swift)); the ZHOOP author rates it "plus or minus 15 to 20%, directional" ([rusheelraj.com](https://www.rusheelraj.com/blog/whoop/)). The 0/1/18/42 weights mean time above threshold dominates Strain far more than Edwards weights imply, so an Edwards clone will overrate long easy sessions and underrate short hard ones. WHOOP's average member logs a daily strain of 10–11, a useful sanity check ([the5krunner](https://the5krunner.com/2022/05/24/whoop-strain-everything/)).

### Muscular load changed Strain twice

Strength Trainer (April 2023) added muscular load from accelerometer and gyroscope data, exercises, reps and weight, with 97% repeatability ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20230425005456/en)). Passive MSK (early 2026) estimates musculoskeletal load from wrist motion without logging and applies it directly to Activity Strain, so new strain values "will be higher"; it feeds Day Strain, Recovery, Sleep Need and Healthspan's strength time and "has not been publicly validated" ([the5krunner](https://the5krunner.com/2026/02/28/new-whoop-strength-trainer-update/); [Gadgets & Wearables](https://gadgetsandwearables.com/2026/02/11/whoop-muscular-load/)). The model uses wrist acceleration, velocity and rotation, body mass and a biomechanical movement model (PUSH heritage), reports Low/Moderate/High, and combines with cardio strain in an undocumented, non-additive way. The API exposes **no muscular-load field** and still describes strain as "based on the user's heart rate." Confidence: **official description, formula unknown**. An HR-only clone will read low on lifting days and, since early 2026, on any day with substantial non-cardio movement. Estimate the effect as the residual between WHOOP strain and your fitted HR-only model on strength workouts.

### HR zones and max HR

WHOOP uses the heart-rate-reserve method, `Target HR = (MaxHR − RHR) × %intensity + RHR`, with a **rolling 14-day RHR baseline** ([WHOOP Locker](https://www.whoop.com/thelocker/more-personalized-heart-rate-zones-with-whoop)). The API reports six zones in ms (`zone_zero_milli` through `zone_five_milli`) for workouts only, without thresholds ([WHOOP OpenAPI](https://api.prod.whoop.com/developer/doc/openapi.json)). The standard boundaries are Z0 below 50%, then 50/60/70/80/90 %HRR, but WHOOP's own Healthspan white paper lists **Z1 as 40–60% HRR** ([WHOOP white paper](https://assets.ctfassets.net/rbzqg6pelgqa/3ONehqJslbqxI7CQlwGjfT/36429d6f66940e1fd866a772ed5bfc93/WHOOP_2025_White_Paper_Healthspan__6_.pdf)); test both lower bounds against `zone_one_milli`. Max HR starts from an estimate using **age, sex and weight** and auto-raises whenever you exceed it; WHOOP calls 220−age inaccurate for individuals ([WHOOP Locker](https://www.whoop.com/thelocker/calculating-max-heart-rate)). The coefficients are unknown, and the Gellish-formula claim from a blogger contradicts WHOOP's own description. The fix is direct: read `max_heart_rate` from `/v2/user/measurement/body`. Because max HR only ratchets upward, one artifact spike can permanently inflate it and deflate Strain; "if you reach a strain of 21 easily, your max heart rate setting is probably wrong" ([the5krunner](https://the5krunner.com/2022/05/24/whoop-strain-everything/)).

### Calories

WHOOP states that it uses BMR and then an "Active Burn" calculation ([WHOOP Locker](https://www.whoop.com/thelocker/calorie-tracking-science/)); a staff reply says BMR comes from height, weight and sex formulas, with calories added by "a proprietary mathematical function related to your heart rate" ([WHOOP Community](https://www.community.whoop.com/t/how-is-calorie-burn-estimated-and-how-accurate-is-it/113)). Max HR changes calories too, so the active term depends on %HRR or %HRmax. Cycle `kilojoule` (example 8288 kJ ≈ 1981 kcal) is consistent with **BMR included over the variable-length cycle**; divide by 4.184 for kcal ([WHOOP OpenAPI](https://api.prod.whoop.com/developer/doc/openapi.json)). A reasonable stand-in is Mifflin-St Jeor BMR (`10·kg + 6.25·cm − 5·age + 5` men, `−161` women) ([Mifflin 1990](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2305711/)) plus Keytel active expenditure above an activity threshold (men `−55.0969 + 0.6309·HR + 0.1988·kg + 0.2017·age` kJ/min; women `−20.4022 + 0.4472·HR − 0.1263·kg + 0.074·age`) ([Keytel 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15966347/)). Keytel returns nonsense at low HR, so switch to BMR below the threshold. Users report WHOOP totals that look low, sometimes below BMR ([WHOOP Community](https://www.community.whoop.com/t/calories-bmr-too-low/5545)), so fit a scale factor and threshold rather than trusting textbook constants. Confidence: **structure official, equations unknown**.

### Stress Monitor and Strain Target

Stress Monitor (March 2023) runs **0 to 3** and compares in-the-moment HR and HRV with "their individual HRV baseline from the last 14 days, as well as their typical resting heart rate," adjusting for motion so exercise is separated from other stressors ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20230329005595/en); [WHOOP Locker](https://www.whoop.com/thelocker/introducing-stress-monitor-a-new-way-to-monitor-manage-stress/)). WHOOP describes 0–1 as very low, 1–2 as alert or focused, 2–3 as highly activated, and good sleep as flat and sometimes below 0.5 ([WHOOP Podcast 215](https://www.whoop.com/us/en/thelocker/podcast-215-introducing-stress-monitor-a-new-way-to-measure-manage-stress/)). The transform is unknown. A design inference is `s = clip(a·(HR − RHR_14d)/sd_HR + b·(lnRMSSD_14d − lnRMSSD)/sd + c, 0, 3)`, with high-motion periods masked. Do not copy openwhoop's Baevsky Stress Index, which outputs 0–10 and is computed from HR rather than RR ([openwhoop stress.rs](https://github.com/ThomasBoutelier/openwhoop/blob/master/src/openwhoop/src/algo/stress.rs)). The Strain Target gives a recommendation "based on your recovery … and strain you've already accumulated," adjustable by intent ([WHOOP Locker](https://www.whoop.com/us/en/thelocker/strain-coach/)); WHOOP's older framework labels recovery-versus-strain combinations as optimal, overreaching or restoring ([WHOOP Locker](https://www.whoop.com/gr/de/thelocker/whoop-training-zones-optimal-overreaching-restoring/)). No numeric mapping exists; one reviewer saw about 15 at 70% recovery ([Pinkbike](https://www.pinkbike.com/news/review-6-months-tracking-recovery-and-strain-with-the-whoop-strap.html)). A monotonic mapping from Recovery to percentiles of the user's own 30–90-day strain distribution is the pragmatic clone. Confidence for both: **structure official, transform unknown**.

## Sleep Need is the one formula you can implement nearly exactly

### Sleep Need and Sleep Debt

The sleep-need patent claims the structure WHOOP ships: a baseline from sleep history, a strain term from the preceding day, a debt term "as an accumulation of prior sleep debt relative to the baseline sleep need over one or more preceding sleep cycles … scaled to minutes, and … capped at a predetermined maximum number of minutes," summed, minus a nap adjustment ([US12318226B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12318226B2/en); [US20240252121A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20240252121A1/en)). Claim 8 gives the strain term `f(i) = 1.7/(1 + e^((17 − i)/3.5))`, i = Day Strain on 0–21. The API's `sleep_needed` object holds exactly four components (`baseline_milli`, `need_from_sleep_debt_milli`, `need_from_recent_strain_milli`, `need_from_recent_nap_milli` ≤ 0) and states that summing them gives total need ([WHOOP API](https://developer.whoop.com/api/)).

The units of 1.7 were ambiguous (a 1.7-*minute* ceiling would be negligible). The API documentation settles it. Its example cycle strain is **5.2951527**, and `1.7/(1 + e^((17 − 5.2951527)/3.5))` = 0.0579432 h = **208,595 ms**, which equals the documented example `need_from_recent_strain_milli` of 208,595 to the millisecond. The examples were evidently generated by the production formula, with **f in hours and i as the prior cycle's Day Strain**. Confidence: **patent claim, verified by inference against API examples**. That gives an extra ~3 minutes at strain 5, 12 minutes at 10, 30 at 14, 51 at 17 and 77 at 21.

```
strain_term_h = 1.7 / (1 + exp((17 − day_strain_prev_cycle) / 3.5))
baseline      = robust central value of sleep over a history window (~28 d community claim),
                using only days with strain below a threshold (claim 14); age/sex prior at start
debt          = min(CAP, sum_k  c_k * max(0, need_k − asleep_k))   # c_k (carryover/decay) and CAP UNKNOWN
nap_term      = −(nap asleep duration) * s                         # Locker implies s ≈ 1
SleepNeed     = baseline + strain_term + debt + nap_term
```

Claim 14 says the baseline uses "a plurality of days … where activity by the user did not exceed a predetermined strain level"; claims 12–13 let debt depend on sleep duration, onset latency, waking intervals and stage time; claim 17 allows age and sex. The Locker says need "is reduced by the amount of time you napped for" ([WHOOP Locker](https://www.whoop.com/us/en/thelocker/how-much-sleep-do-i-need)), and the baseline is learned over roughly the first 28 days per a community report ([WHOOP Community](https://www.community.whoop.com/t/how-do-i-adjust-the-sleep-needed-parameters/8638)). The debt carryover, number of nights and cap are **unknown** but fully recoverable by regressing your exported `need_from_sleep_debt_milli` on lagged shortfalls.

### Sleep Performance changed meaning in 2025

The API defines `sleep_performance_percentage` as "time a user is asleep over the amount of sleep the user needed," 0–100 ([WHOOP API](https://developer.whoop.com/api/)), matching the patent's example of 6 h slept over 8 h needed = 75% ([US9750415B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US9750415B2/en)). WHOOP's support page now says the score blends **sufficiency, consistency, efficiency and sleep stress**, with bands of 85%+ optimal, 70–85% sufficient and below 70% poor ([WHOOP Support](https://support.whoop.com/s/article/WHOOP-Sleep?language=en_US)); a May 2025 community reply dates the update ([WHOOP Community](https://www.community.whoop.com/t/whoop-sleep-consistency/800)). No weights are published. Confidence: **official inputs, unknown blend**. Model it as `min(100, w1·100·min(1, asleep/need) + w2·consistency + w3·efficiency + w4·(100 − sleep_stress_score))` or as sufficiency minus penalties, and fit on post-May-2025 data; compute pure sufficiency against the API field first to learn whether the API value changed definition too.

### Detection, staging, efficiency, restorative sleep and consistency

Onset and wake rules are mostly unpublished. WHOOP-attributed forum replies in 2026 say a sleep stays open unless a wake period of **more than about 50 minutes** occurs, which splits it into a sleep and a nap, and that naps over about an hour "should auto-detect" ([WHOOP Community](https://www.community.whoop.com/t/whoop-wants-me-to-get-out-of-bed-every-time-i-wake-in-the-night-just-so-its-band-can-notice-im-awake-and-incorrect-whoop-terminology-misleading-data/15141); [WHOOP Community](https://www.community.whoop.com/t/auto-detect-naps/7913)). The patent lists temperature drop, lack of activity and falling heart rate as onset signals ([US9750415B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US9750415B2/en)), and the strap firmware already tags each record with a sleep/activity state that openwhoop merges with 15-minute change and 60-minute minimum-sleep constants ([openwhoop activity.rs](https://github.com/ThomasBoutelier/openwhoop/blob/master/src/openwhoop/src/algo/activity.rs)). Confidence: **community-staff**.

Staging is a proprietary PPG-plus-accelerometer model, most likely in 30-s epochs. Its biases are well measured: in Schyvens et al. (WHOOP 4.0, n = 40), κ = 0.37, sleep sensitivity 93.6%, wake specificity only 40.1%, and bias versus PSG of **TST +24 min, efficiency +4.1 points, WASO −19 min, light −29 min, deep +31 min, REM +15 min** ([Schyvens et al. 2025](https://academic.oup.com/sleepadvances/article/6/2/zpaf021/8090472)); Miller et al. found 64% four-stage agreement ([PubMed 32713257](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32713257/)). A more accurate clone will therefore disagree with WHOOP: it will show more wake, more disturbances, less deep and less REM. To match WHOOP rather than PSG, deliberately tilt classification toward sleep and deep.

Efficiency is "time … in bed that you are actually asleep" ([WHOOP API](https://developer.whoop.com/api/)), where "in bed" means the detected sleep bounds; asleep = light + SWS + REM. The documentation example does not resolve whether no-data time leaves the denominator (its components give 90.56% versus a stated 91.70%), so test variants against your data. Restorative sleep is **SWS + REM** ([WHOOP Support](https://support.whoop.com/s/article/WHOOP-Sleep?language=en_US)); the percentage denominator (asleep versus in bed) is unconfirmed. Sleep Consistency is officially "the likelihood that a user's state, whether asleep or awake, is the same at a particular clock time on each of four consecutive days" ([WHOOP Locker](https://www.whoop.com/us/en/thelocker/new-feature-sleep-consistency-why-we-track-it/)); the white paper says it runs "slightly lower than SRI due to the use of a longer baseline period" ([WHOOP white paper](https://assets.ctfassets.net/rbzqg6pelgqa/3ONehqJslbqxI7CQlwGjfT/36429d6f66940e1fd866a772ed5bfc93/WHOOP_2025_White_Paper_Healthspan__6_.pdf)), and the member average is 67.9% ([WHOOP Locker](https://www.whoop.com/us/en/thelocker/research-measuring-sleep-variability/)). Implement it Sleep-Regularity-Index style: bin the last 24 h and prior 4 days into fixed epochs, mark asleep or awake from the staged hypnogram (wake inside sleep counts, which explains 84% scores for identical bedtimes), and compute the share of matching clock-time bins. Epoch size, nap inclusion and pairing are unknown. Do not use openwhoop's coefficient-of-variation approach ([openwhoop sleep_consistency.rs](https://github.com/ThomasBoutelier/openwhoop/blob/master/src/openwhoop/src/algo/sleep_consistency.rs)), which measures something different.

The Sleep Planner recommends a bedtime from tonight's need, a goal of **Peak 100%, Perform 85% or Get By 75%** (Wareable reports 70%, possibly an older value), a wake time, and at least three days of sleep data ([WHOOP Locker](https://www.whoop.com/us/en/thelocker/how-much-sleep-do-i-need); [WHOOP Locker](https://www.whoop.com/au/en/thelocker/feature-update-whoop-app-sleep-planner/); [Wareable](https://www.wareable.com/wearable-tech/how-to-set-an-alarm-on-whoop-4)). The patent notes that time to dedicate to sleep is "somewhat higher" than need after latency and brief wakes ([US9750415B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US9750415B2/en)). Best inference: `bedtime = wake_time − goal × SleepNeed / personal_efficiency − latency`. Smart-alarm modes are exact time, sleep goal (with a latest time) and "in the green" (Recovery ≥ 66%).

## WHOOP Age is the most fully published metric WHOOP has

WHOOP's 2025 Healthspan white paper gives the method nearly end to end ([WHOOP white paper](https://assets.ctfassets.net/rbzqg6pelgqa/3ONehqJslbqxI7CQlwGjfT/36429d6f66940e1fd866a772ed5bfc93/WHOOP_2025_White_Paper_Healthspan__6_.pdf)). WHOOP Age uses **nine contributors**: sleep duration, sleep consistency, steps, HR Zone 1–3 time, HR Zone 4–5 time, strength activity time, VO2max, RHR and lean body mass %. **HRV is not an input.** Each contributor becomes an all-cause mortality hazard ratio against a health-optimized referent at the guideline threshold, then years via **t = ln(HR)/0.1 = 10·ln(HR)** (Spiegelhalter's effective-age method, assuming about 10% more risk per year of age), adjusted by structural-equation factors that remove double counting (for example RHR versus VO2max), and **summed**. The window is the last 6 months and it updates weekly. Missing lean body mass contributes zero. Confidence: **official**, with dose-response curves, SEM factors and caps unknown.

| Contributor | Neutral point (0 years) | Shape | Cited evidence (unadjusted slope) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sleep consistency | 70% | Below adds years, above subtracts | Windred 2024: 20–48% lower mortality (−2.2 to −6.5 y extreme) |
| Sleep duration | 7 h | Below adds; 7–9 h minor benefit; >9 h no effect | Itani 2017 short-sleep HR 1.12 (+1.1 y) |
| HR Zones 1–3 | ~70–100 min/wk, lower with age | Benefit grows to 600 min/wk | Lee 2022 |
| HR Zones 4–5 | ~7–10 min/wk, lower with age | — | Ahmadi 2022: 15–20 min/wk, 18–24% lower |
| Strength time | 40 min/wk | No added benefit above 120 | Momma 2022 |
| Steps | 8,000/day young; 5,600 older | Plateau 8–10k; age interaction | Sheng 2021 −23% per +1k (needs saturation) |
| VO2max | Age/sex table (30 y: 44 M, 38 F) | Above subtracts, below adds | −13% per +1 MET (−1.4 y) |
| RHR (sleep) | ~60 M, ~64 F | Higher adds | +9% per +10 bpm (+0.86 y) |
| Lean mass % | ≥80% M, ≥67% F at 30 | Below adds | +11% per +10 pp fat (+1.0 y) |

The white paper's VO2max minimum targets (mL/kg/min, ages 20 to 100 in 5-year steps) are 40, 40, 38, 36, 34, 32, 30, 29, 27, 26, 25, 23, 22, 21, 20, 19, 18 for women and 46, 46, 44, 42, 40, 37, 36, 34, 32, 30, 29, 27, 26, 25, 23, 22, 21 for men; the age alignment is a best reading of the extracted figure, consistent with the stated age-30 values. Zone time counts only within logged activities, using %HRR zones (Z1 40–60). Strength time counts listed activity types including weightlifting, functional fitness, HIIT, pilates, yoga and rucking. As a test harness, WHOOP's April 2025 median 30-year-old male (7.0 h sleep, 67% consistency, 10,900 steps, 141 min Z1–3, 6 min Z4–5, 46 min strength, VO2max 46.8, RHR 58, lean mass 81%) scores **−1.6 years**, as does the median female (7.3 h, 68%, 11,500, 124, 7, 56, 40.0, 63, 73%), while a typical US adult lands around +6 to +7.5 years ([WHOOP white paper](https://assets.ctfassets.net/rbzqg6pelgqa/3ONehqJslbqxI7CQlwGjfT/36429d6f66940e1fd866a772ed5bfc93/WHOOP_2025_White_Paper_Healthspan__6_.pdf)). Raw slopes overshoot, so your per-component curves must saturate and shrink until these anchors reproduce.

```
WHOOPAge   = age + Σ_i adj_i * 10 * ln( HR_i(x_i_6mo; age, sex) / HR_i(threshold_i) )
PaceOfAging = clip( (WHOOPAge_projected − WHOOPAge_now) / 0.5 , −1, 3 )
  where WHOOPAge_projected = WHOOPAge at age+0.5 with every 6-month average replaced by the last-30-day average
```

Pace of Aging is officially "measured on a scale from −1.0x to 3.0x"; a projected WHOOP Age assumes the recent 30-day averages hold for 6 months, and a 6-month difference equals 1.0x ([WHOOP white paper](https://assets.ctfassets.net/rbzqg6pelgqa/3ONehqJslbqxI7CQlwGjfT/36429d6f66940e1fd866a772ed5bfc93/WHOOP_2025_White_Paper_Healthspan__6_.pdf)); the formula above is the inferred implementation. Advanced Labs biomarkers **do not** change WHOOP Age ([WHOOP Support](https://support.whoop.com/s/article/Healthspan-WHOOP-Age-Pace-of-Aging-Guide?language=en_US)).

VO2max combines a passive model (sex, age, height, weight and wear data), a GPS-augmented model after outdoor runs of 15 minutes or more, and a lab-calibrated anchor; it needs 14 recoveries in 21 days and updates "typically … on Tuesdays" ([WHOOP Support](https://support.whoop.com/s/article/VO2-Max?language=en_US)). WHOOP reports 3.3–3.7 mL/kg/min error against 248 lab tests ([WHOOP Locker](https://www.whoop.com/us/en/thelocker/how-accurate-is-whoop-vo2-max/)). WHOOP never names Uth (`15.3 × HRmax/HRrest`), which remains a stand-in only. Steps arrived with 5.0/MG through "hardware and algorithm changes," with no published method or accuracy ([WHOOP Community](https://www.community.whoop.com/t/since-steps-uses-a-new-algorithm-on-whoop-5-0-mg-is-it-more-accurate/562)); users report undercounting against Apple Watch ([WHOOP Community](https://www.community.whoop.com/t/step-count-massively-inaccurate/6278)). The API now exposes cycle `step_count`, so fit a scale factor rather than reimplementing. Confidence: **unknown** for steps.

Journal Recovery Impacts need **at least 5 Yes and 5 No answers within 90 days**, on days with both a journal entry and a Recovery ([WHOOP Support](https://support.whoop.com/s/article/Recovery-Insights?language=en_US)); another WHOOP page says 10 entries in 30 days ([WHOOP Support](https://support.whoop.com/s/article/WHOOP-Journal-Overview?language=en_US)). The percentage is "an estimate of the behavior's isolated contribution," adjusted for sleep, workouts and other journal behaviors ([WHOOP Locker](https://www.whoop.com/us/en/thelocker/monthly-performance-assessment-now-features-enhanced-recovery-analysis/)). A raw mean(Yes) − mean(No) will not match. Fit a per-user regression `Recovery_next ~ behavior_i + other behaviors + sleep performance + prior strain`, aligning each answer (which describes the previous day) to the following morning's Recovery, and bucket coefficients by size and significance. Confidence: **official structure, model unknown**.

### A per-user calibration plan

Because the decisive constants are unpublished, the reliable route is to treat WHOOP's outputs as labels and fit your model's free parameters per user, one module at a time, freezing each before moving downstream. Start by pulling ground truth through the v2 API (cycles, recoveries, sleeps, workouts, body measurement) plus the CSV export, whose columns include `Recovery score %`, `Heart rate variability (ms)`, `Day Strain`, `Energy burned (cal)`, `Sleep need (min)`, `Sleep debt (min)`, `Sleep consistency %` and per-workout `HR Zone 1–5 %` ([quantifiedme WHOOP loader](https://github.com/ErikBjare/quantifiedme/blob/master/src/quantifiedme/load/whoop.py)). Collect your own raw HR, RR and accelerometer data for the same periods, align everything to WHOOP's cycle and sleep boundaries rather than your own, and keep only `SCORED` records. Hold out the most recent 20–30% of days for testing, and plan for at least 60–90 days, since baselines alone consume weeks.

Fit in dependency order. First, **sleep boundaries and staging**: set your sleep start and end from the API so that downstream comparisons are not polluted, then tune your stager's thresholds to minimize error in stage minutes. Second, **HRV and RHR**: grid-search the SWS weight `a`, the time-of-night function (linear or exponential ramp), linear versus log averaging, and the artifact threshold against `hrv_rmssd_milli`, then do the same for RHR candidates (weighted mean, lowest 5-minute mean, percentile) against `resting_heart_rate`; target a median absolute error of a few ms. Third, **zones and Strain**: fit the zone lower bound (40 versus 50 %HRR) by matching `zone_*_milli` exactly using API max HR; then, on cardio-only workouts recorded before February 2026, fit AT, CPT, N, p (and whether `f(0)` is subtracted) by least squares on Activity Strain, and validate on Day Strain. If the patent form underfits, fall back to `strain = k·ln(c·TRIMP + 1)` with fitted k and c, or NOOP's `ln(D) = 21·Σx²/Σxy` closed form ([NOOP StrainScorer](https://github.com/muftiarfan/noop/blob/main/Packages/StrandAnalytics/Sources/StrandAnalytics/StrainScorer.swift)). Strength-day residuals then estimate the muscular-load contribution. Fourth, **calories**: fit a BMR scale, active-burn slope and HR threshold against workout and cycle `kilojoule`. Fifth, **Sleep Need**: verify that your strain term reproduces `need_from_recent_strain_milli` from the previous cycle's Day Strain (it should match almost exactly), estimate the baseline method from `baseline_milli`, then regress `need_from_sleep_debt_milli` on lagged shortfalls to recover carryover weights and the cap, and check the nap term against `need_from_recent_nap_milli`. Sixth, **sleep scores**: compute sufficiency against `sleep_performance_percentage`, then fit blend weights for consistency, efficiency and sleep stress on post-May-2025 nights; fit efficiency and consistency variants (denominator, epoch size, nap inclusion, pairing) against their API fields. Seventh, **Recovery**: with HRV and RHR now matched, fit baseline half-life, window length, input weights and the logistic or normal-CDF mapping by minimizing error on `recovery_score`, with `user_calibrating` nights excluded. Finally, check WHOOP Age against the white-paper anchors and the app's weekly value.

Judge success by per-metric targets rather than perfection. Reasonable goals are HRV within about 5% median absolute error, Strain within 0.5 points, Sleep Need within 10 minutes, and Recovery within 5–8 points (the single-user regression's 10-point residual is the floor to beat). Refit every few months and after any WHOOP announcement, because WHOOP retrains its heart-rate algorithm regularly ([WHOOP Locker](https://www.whoop.com/eu/en/thelocker/a-look-behind-the-data-how-whoop-measures-heart-rate)) and reprocesses history. Constants fitted on one user transfer poorly to others: the clone should ship the fitting pipeline, not one person's numbers.

## Conclusion

The search for WHOOP's "exact formulas" mostly ends at a split: WHOOP publishes the architecture of its scores and withholds the constants. That split is useful for a clone, because architecture causes the large errors and constants cause the small ones. Day boundaries, the HRV window, baseline normalization, max HR, the strain weighting and the 2025–2026 additions (blended Sleep Performance, passive muscular load) are all documented well enough to fix outright, and fixing them should remove most of the systematic gap before any fitting begins. The sleep-need strain term goes further: it is an exact, verifiable formula, and its millisecond agreement with WHOOP's own API example shows that patent equations can describe production code even where WHOOP never confirms it.

What remains after the structural fixes is a parameter-estimation problem, not a research problem. Treat every WHOOP field the API exposes as a label, fit the unknowns in dependency order, and accept a small irreducible residual from WHOOP's richer raw-PPG pipeline and its periodic retraining. A clone that is calibrated per user against WHOOP's own outputs will track WHOOP more closely than any of the open-source projects, which have never been calibrated against WHOOP at all.
